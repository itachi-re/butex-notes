# Q. What do you mean by Grignard reagents and TEL? Explain the method of preparation and use.

---

## 1. Grignard Reagent

**Definition**
A Grignard reagent is an organomagnesium halide, represented as **R–MgX**, formed by reacting an alkyl or aryl halide with magnesium metal in dry ether. Here R = alkyl/aryl group, X = Cl, Br, or I.

### Preparation (general)

$$R-X + Mg \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} R-MgX$$

**Conditions required (important for exam):**
- Solvent must be **absolutely dry ether** (or THF) — even trace moisture destroys the reagent.
- Reaction carried out under an **inert atmosphere** (N₂), since Grignard reagents also react with atmospheric O₂ and CO₂.
- Reactivity of halides: **R–I > R–Br > R–Cl**.

<details>
<summary><strong>Preparation examples</strong></summary>

$$CH_3CH_2-Br + Mg \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} CH_3CH_2-MgBr \quad \text{(Ethylmagnesium bromide)}$$

$$C_6H_5-Br + Mg \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} C_6H_5-MgBr \quad \text{(Phenylmagnesium bromide)}$$

$$CH_3-I + Mg \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} CH_3-MgI \quad \text{(Methylmagnesium iodide)}$$

</details>

### Uses of Grignard Reagent

| Reacts with | Product (after H₃O⁺ workup) |
|---|---|
| Formaldehyde (HCHO) | Primary alcohol |
| Other aldehydes (RCHO) | Secondary alcohol |
| Ketones (R₂C=O) | Tertiary alcohol |
| CO₂ (dry ice) | Carboxylic acid |
| Water / alcohol / acid (any active H) | Alkane (R–H) |

**General mechanism (with aldehyde/ketone):**

$$R-MgX + R'_2C=O \longrightarrow R'_2C(OMgX)-R \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} R'_2C(OH)-R$$

<details>
<summary><strong>Worked reaction examples</strong></summary>

**(a) With formaldehyde → primary alcohol**

$$CH_3MgBr + HCHO \longrightarrow CH_3-CH_2-OMgBr \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} CH_3CH_2OH \; (\text{Ethanol})$$

**(b) With acetaldehyde → secondary alcohol**

$$CH_3MgBr + CH_3CHO \longrightarrow CH_3-CH(OMgBr)-CH_3 \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} CH_3CH(OH)CH_3 \; (\text{Isopropanol})$$

**(c) With acetone → tertiary alcohol**

$$CH_3MgBr + (CH_3)_2C=O \longrightarrow (CH_3)_3C-OMgBr \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} (CH_3)_3C-OH \; (\text{tert-Butanol})$$

**(d) With CO₂ (dry ice) → carboxylic acid**

$$CH_3MgBr + CO_2 \longrightarrow CH_3COOMgBr \xrightarrow{H_3O^+} CH_3COOH \; (\text{Acetic acid})$$

**(e) With water (active hydrogen) → alkane**

$$CH_3MgBr + H_2O \longrightarrow CH_4 + Mg(OH)Br$$

*(Shows why the reagent must be kept away from moisture — it is destroyed instead of doing useful chemistry.)*

</details>

Grignard reagents are important in organic synthesis for building new **C–C bonds** and preparing alcohols, carboxylic acids, and hydrocarbons.

## 2. TEL (Tetraethyl Lead)

**Definition**
TEL is Pb(C₂H₅)₄, an organolead compound used as an **anti-knock additive** in petrol/gasoline.

### Preparation (from Grignard reagent)

$$4\,C_2H_5MgCl + PbCl_2 \longrightarrow Pb(C_2H_5)_4 + 4\,MgCl_2$$

Four ethyl groups from four molecules of ethylmagnesium chloride replace the two Cl atoms and add two more ethyl groups on lead, giving tetraethyllead.

*(Industrially, TEL was also produced from ethyl chloride + sodium-lead alloy, but the Grignard-based equation above is the expected exam answer since it directly links to Grignard chemistry.)*

### Use

A small amount of TEL was blended into petrol as an anti-knock agent. On heating in the engine cylinder, it decomposes into lead atoms and ethyl free radicals:

$$Pb(C_2H_5)_4 \xrightarrow{\Delta} Pb + 4\,C_2H_5^{\bullet}$$

These radicals interrupt the uncontrolled radical chain reactions responsible for engine knocking, allowing smoother combustion and effectively raising the fuel's **octane number**.

> **Note (extra mark):** TEL is now banned/phased out globally because it releases toxic lead into the environment via exhaust gases, causing lead poisoning and air pollution.

---

## One-line Summary

> Grignard reagents (RMgX) are versatile organomagnesium compounds used to form C–C bonds in organic synthesis, prepared from alkyl halides and Mg in dry ether; TEL, Pb(C₂H₅)₄, is a practical industrial application of this chemistry, prepared from a Grignard reagent and used as an anti-knock agent in petrol.
