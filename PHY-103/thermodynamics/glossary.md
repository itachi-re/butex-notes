# Glossary — Thermodynamics (Part 1)

**Adiabatic index ($\gamma$)** — The ratio $C_P/C_V$ of a gas's molar heat capacities; governs the steepness of adiabatic P–V curves.

**Adiabatic process** — A process in which no heat is exchanged with the surroundings ($Q=0$).

**Boundary** — The real or imaginary surface separating a thermodynamic system from its surroundings.

**Closed system** — A system that can exchange energy but not matter with its surroundings.

**Cyclic process** — A sequence of processes that returns a system exactly to its initial state.

**Degrees of freedom (f)** — The independent modes (translational, rotational, vibrational) in which a molecule can store energy.

**Dissipation** — The irreversible conversion of organized (mechanical) energy into disordered thermal energy, typically via friction or viscosity.

**Equilibrium (thermodynamic)** — A condition of simultaneous mechanical, thermal, and chemical balance in which a system's state variables are well defined and unchanging.

**Equipartition theorem** — The principle that each quadratic degree of freedom of a molecule contributes, on average, $\tfrac12k_BT$ of energy.

**Extensive property** — A property whose value scales with the amount of matter in the system (e.g. volume, internal energy, mass).

**First Law of Thermodynamics** — The statement of energy conservation for thermodynamic systems: $\Delta Q = \Delta U + W$.

**Free expansion** — Expansion of a gas into a vacuum with no opposing external pressure; a canonical irreversible process with $Q=W=\Delta U=0$.

**Intensive property** — A property independent of the amount of matter in the system (e.g. pressure, temperature, density).

**Internal energy (U)** — The total microscopic kinetic and potential energy of a system's constituent particles; a state function.

**Irreversible process** — A process that cannot be exactly reversed without producing a permanent change somewhere in the universe.

**Isobaric process** — A process occurring at constant pressure.

**Isochoric (isometric) process** — A process occurring at constant volume.

**Isolated system** — A system that exchanges neither matter nor energy with its surroundings.

**Isothermal process** — A process occurring at constant temperature.

**Mayer's relation** — The relation $C_P - C_V = R$ for one mole of an ideal gas.

**Mechanical equivalent of heat (J)** — The conversion factor between mechanical work and heat, $W=JQ$, historically $\approx4.186$ J/cal.

**Molar heat capacity** — Heat capacity per mole of substance (J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹); denoted $C_V$ (constant volume) or $C_P$ (constant pressure).

**Open system** — A system that can exchange both matter and energy with its surroundings.

**Path function** — A quantity (e.g. heat, work) whose value depends on the specific process/path taken between two states, not just the endpoints.

**Quasi-static process** — A process carried out infinitely slowly, so the system remains arbitrarily close to equilibrium throughout.

**Reversible process** — An idealized process that can be exactly retraced, restoring both the system and its surroundings to their original states with no net effect anywhere.

**Specific heat capacity** — Heat capacity per unit mass of substance (J·kg⁻¹·K⁻¹); related to molar heat capacity by $C=Mc$.

**State function** — A property (e.g. $P$, $V$, $T$, $U$) that depends only on the current equilibrium state of a system, not on how that state was reached.

**State variable / thermodynamic coordinate** — An independent variable (typically $P$, $V$, $T$) used to specify the equilibrium state of a system.

**Surroundings** — Everything external to a system that can potentially interact with it.

**System (thermodynamic)** — A specific portion of matter or region of space chosen for thermodynamic study, set apart from its surroundings by a boundary.

**Universe (thermodynamic)** — The combination of a system and its surroundings.

**Work (W)** — Energy transferred across a system's boundary via a macroscopic force acting through a displacement (e.g. $P\,dV$ for a gas); a path function.
