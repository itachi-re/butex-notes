# Oxalic Acid — Identification

## Aim

To identify oxalic acid (HOOC–COOH) by its physical properties and characteristic chemical tests.

## Principle / Theory

Oxalic acid is identified by its oxidation with acidified KMnO₄ (decolourisation, evolution of CO₂), its decomposition on heating with concentrated H₂SO₄, and precipitation with calcium chloride.

## Required Reagents

- Oxalic acid (anhydrous and dihydrate, HOOC–COOH·2H₂O)
- Potassium permanganate (KMnO₄)
- Dilute H₂SO₄
- Concentrated H₂SO₄
- Calcium chloride (CaCl₂)

## Apparatus

- Test tubes

## Physical Properties (from source)

Anhydrous oxalic acid (HOOC–COOH) is a colourless, odourless, white powder with a marked acid taste. It crystallises from water as the dihydrate (HOOC–COOH·2H₂O) in the form of colourless prismatic crystals. The melting point of the hydrated acid is 101.5°C, while that of the anhydrous acid is 189.5°C. It is soluble in water and ethanol, and practically insoluble in ether. It is an active poison.

## Procedure

1. **Oxidation with KMnO₄:** Treat oxalic acid with potassium permanganate and dilute sulphuric acid; heat to 60°C.
2. **Action with concentrated H₂SO₄:** Heat oxalic acid with concentrated H₂SO₄.
3. **Action with CaCl₂:** Add calcium chloride solution to oxalic acid.

## Chemical Reactions

**1. Oxidation with acidified KMnO₄ (at 60°C)**

$$
2KMnO_4 + 5(COOH)_2 + 3H_2SO_4 \xrightarrow{60^\circ C} 10CO_2\uparrow + K_2SO_4 + 2MnSO_4 + 8H_2O
$$

The purple colour of the KMnO₄ solution disappears. (This test is restated in the source's summary section in equivalent form: $5\,HOOC\text{-}COOH + 3H_2SO_4 + 2KMnO_4 \xrightarrow{\Delta,\ 60^\circ C} 10CO_2\uparrow + K_2SO_4 + 2MnSO_4 + 8H_2O$.)

**2. Action with concentrated H₂SO₄ (heat)**

$$
HOOC\text{-}COOH \xrightarrow{conc.\ H_2SO_4,\ \Delta} CO_2 + CO + H_2O
$$

**3. Action with CaCl₂**

$$
HOOC\text{-}COOH + CaCl_2 \rightarrow (COO)_2Ca\downarrow + 2HCl
$$

## Observations

| Test | Reagent/Condition | Observation | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oxidation test | KMnO₄ + dil. H₂SO₄, 60°C | Purple colour of KMnO₄ disappears; CO₂ evolved | Positive — reducing dicarboxylic acid |
| Action with conc. H₂SO₄ | Heat | Decomposes into CO₂, CO, and H₂O | Confirms oxalic acid decomposition behaviour |
| Action with CaCl₂ | CaCl₂ solution | White precipitate of calcium oxalate, (COO)₂Ca | Positive |

## Precautions

- Oxalic acid is an active poison — handle with care.
- The calcium oxalate precipitate is soluble in HCl but insoluble in CH₃COOH; useful for confirming identity.
