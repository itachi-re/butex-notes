# Acetic Acid — Identification

## Aim

To identify acetic acid (CH₃COOH) by its physical properties and characteristic chemical tests.

## Principle / Theory

Acetic acid is identified through ester formation with ethanol (fruity smell), precipitation as silver acetate with AgNO₃, its behaviour with Denigè's solution and with acidified KMnO₄, and the cacodyl oxide test with arsenious oxide.

## Required Reagents

- Acetic acid (CH₃COOH)
- Ethyl alcohol (C₂H₅OH)
- Concentrated H₂SO₄
- Silver nitrate (AgNO₃)
- Sodium hydroxide (NaOH)
- Denigè's solution
- Dilute H₂SO₄, KMnO₄
- Arsenious oxide (As₂O₃)

## Apparatus

- Test tubes

## Physical Properties (from source)

Acetic acid is a colourless liquid with a sharp "vinegar" odour and sour taste, bp 118.1°C. When it forms "ice-like" crystals which melt at 16.7°C, hence the pure anhydrous acid is usually called **glacial acetic acid** (glacial = of ice). It has a corrosive action on the skin and causes blisters. It is miscible with water, ethanol, and ether in all proportions.

## Procedure

1. **Ester formation:** CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH, concentrated H₂SO₄, heat.
2. **Silver nitrate test:** CH₃COOH + AgNO₃, add NaOH dropwise.
3. **Denigè's solution test:** CH₃COOH + Denigè's solution + NaOH (dropwise).
4. **KMnO₄ test:** CH₃COOH + dilute H₂SO₄ + KMnO₄ + NaOH (dropwise).
5. **Cacodyl oxide test:** CH₃COOH + NaOH + As₂O₃, heat.

## Chemical Reactions

**1. Ester formation (ethyl acetate)**

$$
CH_3COOH + C_2H_5OH \xrightarrow{conc.\ H_2SO_4,\ \Delta} CH_3COOC_2H_5 + H_2O
$$

Sweet-smelling ethyl ester.

**2. Silver nitrate test**

$$
CH_3COOH + NaOH \rightarrow CH_3COONa + H_2O
$$

$$
CH_3COONa + AgNO_3 \rightarrow CH_3COOAg\downarrow + NaNO_3
$$

White precipitate.

**3. Denigè's solution test**

$$
CH_3COOH + \text{Denigè's soln} + NaOH \text{ (dropwise)} \rightarrow \text{no precipitate}
$$

**4. KMnO₄ test**

$$
CH_3COOH + dil.\ H_2SO_4 + KMnO_4 + NaOH \text{ (dropwise)} \rightarrow \text{colour of KMnO}_4\text{ does not change}
$$

**5. Cacodyl oxide test**

$$
4CH_3COOH + 4NaOH + As_2O_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} (CH_3)_2As\text{-}O\text{-}As(CH_3)_2 + 2CO_2 + 2Na_2CO_3
$$

Harmful cacodyl oxide gas evolved.

## Observations

| Test | Reagent/Condition | Observation | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ester formation | C₂H₅OH + conc. H₂SO₄, Δ | Sweet smell of ethyl acetate | Positive for –COOH |
| Silver nitrate test | AgNO₃ + NaOH (dropwise) | White precipitate of silver acetate | Positive |
| Denigè's solution test | Denigè's soln + NaOH | No precipitate | Negative / distinguishing result |
| KMnO₄ test | dil. H₂SO₄ + KMnO₄ + NaOH | Colour of KMnO₄ does not disappear | Distinguishes acetic acid (non-reducing) from oxalic/formic acid |
| Cacodyl oxide test | NaOH + As₂O₃, heat | Harmful gas evolved (cacodyl oxide) | Confirmatory, specific to acetate |

## Precautions

- Acetic acid has a corrosive action on the skin and causes blisters — handle with care.
- The cacodyl oxide test produces a harmful gas; perform with adequate ventilation.

## Notes / Observations from Source

- The KMnO₄ test result (no colour change) is a key distinguishing feature from oxalic acid and formic acid, both of which decolourise KMnO₄.
