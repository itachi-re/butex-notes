## Determination of % Error

$$
\%\,\text{error} = \frac{\text{Actual conc. of KMnO}_4 - \text{Experimental conc. of KMnO}_4}{\text{Actual conc. of KMnO}_4} \times 100
$$

$$
= \frac{0.02 - 0.0199}{0.02} \times 100 = 0.5\%
$$

> **N.B.:** After the experiment, wash the burette with FeSO₄ solution.

## Experiment 3: Estimation of Fe²⁺ with Standard KMnO₄ Solution

### Essential Chemicals

- KMnO₄
- Mohr's salt — FeSO₄·(NH₄)₂SO₄·6H₂O
- Green vitriol — FeSO₄·7H₂O
- 1 M H₂SO₄

### Essential Equipment

Burette, pipette, volumetric flask, conical flask, funnel, pipette filler, etc.

### Molecular Weights

| Compound | Molecular Weight (g/mol) |
|---|---:|
| KMnO₄ | 158 |
| FeSO₄·(NH₄)₂SO₄·6H₂O (Mohr's salt) | 392 |
| FeSO₄·7H₂O | 278 |

---

## Preparation of Solutions

### Step 1: Preparation of 500 mL 0.02 M KMnO₄ Solution

1000 mL of 0.02 M KMnO₄ solution contains:

$$
158 \times 0.02 = 3.16 \text{ g KMnO}_4
$$

Therefore, 500 mL of 0.02 M KMnO₄ solution contains:

$$
\frac{158 \times 500 \times 0.02}{1000} = 1.58 \text{ g KMnO}_4
$$

**Actual concentration of KMnO₄:**

$$
\frac{0.0206}{0.0200} \times 0.02 \approx 0.0206 \text{ M}
$$

> **Note:** The exact handwritten value is unclear.

### Step 2: Preparation of 500 mL 1 M H₂SO₄ Solution

Using concentrated H₂SO₄ of approximately 18 M:

$$
V_1 M_1 = V_2 M_2
$$

$$
V_1 \times 18 = 500 \times 1
$$

$$
V_1 = \frac{500}{18} = 27.77 \text{ mL}
$$

Therefore, **27.77 mL of concentrated H₂SO₄** is required and the solution is made up to **500 mL**.

### Step 3: Preparation of 250 mL 0.1 M Mohr's Salt Solution

For FeSO₄·(NH₄)₂SO₄·6H₂O:

$$
\text{Required mass} = \frac{392 \times 250 \times 0.1}{1000} = 9.8 \text{ g}
$$

**Handwritten procedure:** Take **10 g Mohr's salt** in a **250 mL volumetric flask**, dissolve it in **200 mL of 1 M H₂SO₄**, and then make up the volume with **50 mL H₂O**.

## Step 4: Titration of Ferrous Salt Solution with Standard KMnO₄

Take **20 mL ferrous salt solution** and add approximately **5 mL conc. H₂SO₄**. Titrate against standard KMnO₄ solution from the burette.

**End point:** Light violet/pink colour persists.

> **N.B.:** Conc. H₂SO₄ is added to prevent oxidation of Fe²⁺ by air.

### Burette Readings

| Trial | Initial Reading (mL) | Final Reading (mL) | Difference (mL) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.0 | 4.0 | 4.0 |
| 2 | 4.0 | 8.1 | 4.1 |
| 3 | 8.1 | 12.0 | 3.9 |

Average titre value:

$$
\frac{4.0 + 4.1 + 3.9}{3} = 4.0 \text{ mL}
$$

## Chemistry–II: Calculations

### Reactions

**Decomposition of KMnO₄ in Acidic Medium**

$$
2KMnO_4 + 3H_2SO_4 \rightarrow K_2SO_4 + 2MnSO_4 + 3H_2O + 5[O]
$$

**Oxidation of Ferrous Sulphate**

$$
10FeSO_4 + 5H_2SO_4 + 5[O] \rightarrow 5Fe_2(SO_4)_3 + 5H_2O
$$

**Combined Molecular Equation**

$$
2KMnO_4 + 8H_2SO_4 + 10FeSO_4 \rightarrow K_2SO_4 + 2MnSO_4 + 5Fe_2(SO_4)_3 + 8H_2O
$$

### Ionic Equation

$$
2MnO_4^- + 16H^+ + 10Fe^{2+} \rightarrow 2Mn^{2+} + 10Fe^{3+} + 8H_2O
$$

**Half-Reactions**

Reduction:

$$
2MnO_4^- + 16H^+ + 10e^- \rightarrow 2Mn^{2+} + 8H_2O
$$

Oxidation:

$$
10Fe^{2+} \rightarrow 10Fe^{3+} + 10e^-
$$
