# Estimation of Washing Soda (Na₂CO₃ Content)

## Aim

Determination of the Na₂CO₃ content of washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O) by acid–base titration.

## Required Reagents

- Washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O)
- 0.1N HCl solution
- Methyl orange indicator

## Apparatus

- Volumetric flask
- Conical flask
- Burette
- Pipette
- Funnel

## Procedure

### Step 1: Preparation of 250 ml 0.1N washing soda solution

- Molecular weight of washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O) = 106 + 180 = 286
- Equivalent weight of washing soda = molecular weight / (number of metal atoms × valency of metal) = 286 / (2×1) = 143
- 1000 ml of 1N washing soda solution contains 143 g washing soda
- ∴ 250 ml of 0.1N solution requires: 143 × 250 × 0.1 / 1000 = 3.575 g

> **Source note:** The handwritten source shows an additional figure ("≈ 3.68 g") alongside the calculated 3.575 g. This discrepancy is reproduced as written rather than corrected; the second figure is marked [unclear in source].

- ∴ 1000 ml of 0.1N washing soda solution requires: 14.3 g

### Step 2: Preparation of 250 ml 0.1N HCl solution

Using S₁V₁ (conc. HCl) = S₂V₂ (dilute HCl):

$$
V_1 = \frac{\text{Required volume of HCl} \times \text{Required strength of HCl}}{\text{Strength of conc. HCl}} = \frac{250 \times 0.1N}{12N} = 2.08\ \text{ml of conc. HCl}
$$

### Step 3: Acid–Base Titration

Titration of washing soda solution against standard 0.1N HCl, using methyl orange indicator.

| Trial | Volume of washing soda (ml) | Burette 1st reading (ml) | Burette final reading (ml) | Difference (ml) | Average reading (ml) | Strength of washing soda |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 ml + 20 ml H₂O | 0 | 20.20 | 20.20 | | |
| 2 | + 1–2 drops methyl orange | 20.20 | 40.50 | 20.30 | 20.2 | 0.101 N |
| 3 | Add 0.1N HCl soln → orange colour (endpoint) | 0 | 20.21 | 20.21 | | |

> The three rows above are reproduced as tabulated in the source, which records the procedural notes alongside the readings rather than as separate repeated trials.

### Step 4: Calculation

Reaction:

$$
2HCl + Na_2CO_3 \rightarrow 2NaCl + CO_2 + H_2O
$$

For washing soda vs. HCl (Na₂CO₃ is dibasic):

$$
S_1V_1(\text{washing soda}) = \frac{1}{2}\,S_2V_2(\text{HCl})
$$

$$
\therefore S_1 = \frac{S_2V_2(\text{HCl})}{V_1 \times 2} = \frac{20.2 \times 0.1N}{10 \times 2} = 0.101\ N
$$

Equivalent weight of Na₂CO₃ = 106 / 2 = 53

- 1000 ml of 1N Na₂CO₃ solution contains 53 g Na₂CO₃
- 1000 ml of 0.101N solution contains: 53 × 0.101 = 5.353 g Na₂CO₃
- 14.3 g washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O) contains 5.353 g Na₂CO₃
- ∴ 100 g washing soda contains: (5.353 × 100) / 14.3 = **37.43% Na₂CO₃**

## Result

Washing soda (as supplied) contains **37.43% Na₂CO₃**.

## Notes / Observations from Source

> N.B. (source): Washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O) typically contains 35% to 40% Na₂CO₃ — the experimental result (37.43%) falls within this expected range.
