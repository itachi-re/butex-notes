# Chemistry-II Practical

## Experiment: Identification of Organic Compounds (Carboxylic Acids)

### Objective
To identify the given organic compound as one of the four carboxylic acids — formic acid, acetic acid, oxalic acid, or benzoic acid — using characteristic qualitative tests.

### Compounds Identified
1. Formic acid (HCOOH)
2. Acetic acid (CH₃COOH)
3. Oxalic acid (HOOC–COOH)
4. Benzoic acid (C₆H₅COOH)

### General Test for –COOH Group (common to all)

**Reagent:** NaHCO₃ (or Na₂CO₃)

**Observation:** Brisk effervescence due to CO₂ gas.

$$
\mathrm{HCOOH + NaHCO_3 \rightarrow HCOONa + H_2O + CO_2\uparrow}
$$

**Inference:** Confirms presence of a carboxylic acid (–COOH) group but does not distinguish between them.

---

## 1. Identification of Formic Acid (HCOOH)

### Physical Properties
Colourless liquid, pungent smell, hygroscopic; soluble in water, alcohol and ether; irritating to mucous membranes, eyes and skin. Present in ant/bee stings.

### Test 1: Conc. H₂SO₄ Test

**Reagent:** Conc. H₂SO₄

**Condition:** Heat

**Observation:** Colourless gas evolved, burns with a blue flame.

$$
\mathrm{HCOOH \xrightarrow[\Delta]{conc.\,H_2SO_4} CO\uparrow + H_2O}
$$

**Inference:** Formic acid decomposes to give CO gas — this dehydration test distinguishes it from other carboxylic acids.

### Test 2: Fehling's Solution Test

**Reagent:** Fehling's solution (Cu²⁺ complex, ≈ 2Cu(OH)₂)

**Condition:** Heat

**Observation:** Red precipitate of Cu₂O forms.

$$
\mathrm{HCOOH + 2Cu(OH)_2 \rightarrow Cu_2O\downarrow(red) + CO_2\uparrow + 3H_2O}
$$

**Inference:** Formic acid has an aldehyde-like H attached to –COOH, giving it reducing (aldehydic) character — positive Fehling's test, unlike other carboxylic acids.

### Test 3: KMnO₄ Test

**Reagent:** Dilute KMnO₄ acidified with dilute H₂SO₄

**Observation:** Purple colour of KMnO₄ decolourises.

$$
\mathrm{2KMnO_4 + 3H_2SO_4 + 5HCOOH \rightarrow K_2SO_4 + 2MnSO_4 + 5CO_2\uparrow + 8H_2O}
$$

**Inference:** Formic acid is readily oxidised to CO₂ and H₂O by acidified KMnO₄.

### Test 4: Tollens' Reagent Test

**Reagent:** Tollens' reagent, [Ag(NH₃)₂]OH

**Condition:** Warm in a test tube

**Observation:** Bright silver mirror deposits on the walls of the tube.

$$
\mathrm{HCOOH + 2[Ag(NH_3)_2]OH \rightarrow 2Ag\downarrow + (NH_4)_2CO_3 + 2NH_3 + H_2O}
$$

**Inference:** Reducing (aldehydic) property of formic acid gives a positive silver-mirror test.

### Identification
Formic acid is the only one of the four acids that reduces **Fehling's solution** and **Tollens' reagent**, gives **CO gas** with conc. H₂SO₄, and readily decolourises **cold** acidified KMnO₄.

## 2. Identification of Acetic Acid (CH₃COOH)

### Test 1: Silver Nitrate Test

**Reagent:** NaOH (added dropwise to neutralise), then AgNO₃

**Observation:** White precipitate of silver acetate.

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COOH + NaOH \rightarrow CH_3COONa + H_2O}
$$

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COONa + AgNO_3 \rightarrow CH_3COOAg\downarrow(white) + NaNO_3}
$$

**Inference:** Confirms acetate ion.

### Test 2: Esterification Test

**Reagent:** Ethyl alcohol (C₂H₅OH) + conc. H₂SO₄

**Condition:** Heat

**Observation:** Sweet, fruity smell of ethyl acetate.

$$
\mathrm{CH_3COOH + C_2H_5OH \xrightarrow[\Delta]{conc.\,H_2SO_4} CH_3COOC_2H_5 + H_2O}
$$

**Inference:** Characteristic ester formation confirms the –COOH group with an alcohol.

### Test 3: KMnO₄ Test

**Reagent:** Dilute KMnO₄ + dilute H₂SO₄

**Observation:** Colour of KMnO₄ does **not** disappear under ordinary (cold) conditions.

**Inference:** Unlike formic and oxalic acid, acetic acid is not readily oxidised by cold acidified KMnO₄ — a negative result here helps distinguish acetic acid.

### Identification
Acetic acid gives a **white silver acetate precipitate**, a **fruity ester smell** on esterification, and does **not** decolourise cold KMnO₄.

## 3. Identification of Oxalic Acid (HOOC–COOH)

### Physical Properties
Anhydrous oxalic acid is a colourless, odourless, white powder with a marked acid taste. It crystallises from water as the dihydrate (HOOC–COOH·2H₂O) in colourless prismatic crystals. Melting point of the hydrate ≈ 101 °C; anhydrous acid ≈ 189.5 °C. Soluble in water and alcohol; practically insoluble in ether. It is poisonous.

### Test 1: KMnO₄ Oxidation Test

**Reagent:** Dilute KMnO₄ acidified with dilute H₂SO₄

**Condition:** Heat to ≈ 60 °C

**Observation:** Purple colour of KMnO₄ decolourises.

$$
\mathrm{2KMnO_4 + 5H_2C_2O_4 + 3H_2SO_4 \rightarrow K_2SO_4 + 2MnSO_4 + 10CO_2\uparrow + 8H_2O}
$$

**Inference:** Oxalic acid is oxidised to CO₂ and water; this reaction is also used to standardise KMnO₄ solutions.

### Test 2: Conc. H₂SO₄ (Decomposition) Test

**Reagent:** Conc. H₂SO₄

**Condition:** Heat

**Observation:** Colourless gas mixture evolved (CO and CO₂).

$$
\mathrm{HOOC{-}COOH \xrightarrow[\Delta]{conc.\,H_2SO_4} CO_2\uparrow + CO\uparrow + H_2O}
$$

**Inference:** Characteristic decomposition (dehydration/decarbonylation) of oxalic acid.

### Test 3: Calcium Chloride Test

**Reagent:** CaCl₂ solution

**Observation:** White precipitate of calcium oxalate forms; the precipitate is **insoluble in acetic acid (CH₃COOH)** but **soluble in mineral acid (HCl)**.

$$
\mathrm{HOOC{-}COOH + CaCl_2 \rightarrow (COO)_2Ca\downarrow(white) + 2HCl}
$$

**Inference:** Confirms the oxalate ion; the solubility behaviour distinguishes calcium oxalate from calcium carbonate (which dissolves readily in acetic acid).

### Identification
Oxalic acid decolourises acidified KMnO₄ **on heating (≈60 °C)**, gives a **CO + CO₂** mixture with conc. H₂SO₄, and forms a **calcium oxalate precipitate** (insoluble in CH₃COOH, soluble in HCl).

## 4. Identification of Benzoic Acid (C₆H₅COOH)

### Test 1: NaOH + AgNO₃ Test

**Reagent:** NaOH, then AgNO₃

**Observation:** White precipitate of silver benzoate.

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH + NaOH \rightarrow C_6H_5COONa + H_2O}
$$

$$
\mathrm{C_6H_5COONa + AgNO_3 \rightarrow C_6H_5COOAg\downarrow(white) + NaNO_3}
$$

### Test 2: Hot/Cold Solubility Test

**Reagent:** Dissolve in NaOH (clear solution), then acidify with dilute HCl

**Observation:** White precipitate of benzoic acid re-forms (sparingly soluble in cold water); the precipitate **dissolves on heating** (soluble in hot water) and **reappears on cooling**.

**Inference:** This reversible hot/cold solubility behaviour is characteristic of benzoic acid and is used for its identification and recrystallisation.

### Identification
Benzoic acid gives a **white silver benzoate precipitate** with NaOH/AgNO₃, and shows the distinctive **soluble-in-hot, precipitates-in-cold water** behaviour.

## Important CT Points

- All carboxylic acids give effervescence with NaHCO₃ — this test alone cannot distinguish between them.
- **Formic acid** is unique in giving positive **Fehling's** and **Tollens'** tests due to its aldehydic (–CHO-like) hydrogen; it also readily decolourises **cold** KMnO₄.
- **Acetic acid** does **not** decolourise cold KMnO₄ and forms **silver acetate** with AgNO₃; its ester with ethanol has a sweet fruity smell.
- **Oxalic acid** requires **heating (~60 °C)** to decolourise KMnO₄, and gives a **CO + CO₂** mixture on decomposition with conc. H₂SO₄.
- **Calcium oxalate** precipitate is insoluble in acetic acid but soluble in mineral acids (HCl) — this distinguishes it from calcium carbonate.
- **Benzoic acid** is only sparingly soluble in cold water but freely soluble in hot water — precipitates back on cooling (basis of recrystallisation and identification).
