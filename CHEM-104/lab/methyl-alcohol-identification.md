# Methyl Alcohol (MeOH) — Identification

## Aim

To identify methyl alcohol (MeOH) by characteristic chemical tests.

## Principle / Theory

Methyl alcohol is identified by a set of qualitative tests: ester formation with salicylic acid (methyl salicylate), oxidation to formaldehyde on a heated copper gauze, and — most decisively — a **negative** iodoform test, which is the key test used to distinguish methanol from ethanol.

## Required Reagents

- Methyl alcohol (CH₃OH)
- Iodine (I₂), resublimed
- Salicylic acid
- Concentrated H₂SO₄
- NaOH / KOH
- Anhydrous CH₃COOH

## Apparatus

- Test tubes
- Red-hot copper gauze (for the oxidation test)

## Procedure

1. **Methyl Salicylate Test:** In a test tube, add 2 ml MeOH to salicylic acid and concentrated H₂SO₄; heat (Δ).
2. **Formaldehyde Test:** Take 2–3 ml MeOH in a test tube and heat it with a red-hot copper gauze.
3. **Iodoform Test:** Add MeOH to NaOH and iodine (I₂).
4. **Acetate (Ester) Formation:** Treat MeOH with NaOH/KOH and anhydrous CH₃COOH; separately, MeOH is heated with CH₃COOH and concentrated H₂SO₄.

## Chemical Reactions

**1. Methyl salicylate test**

$$
CH_3OH + \text{(salicylic acid, o-HOC}_6H_4\text{COOH)} \xrightarrow{conc.\ H_2SO_4,\ \Delta} \text{methyl salicylate (o-HOC}_6H_4\text{COOCH}_3\text{)} + H_2O
$$

**2. Formaldehyde test**

$$
CH_3OH + [O] \rightarrow HCHO + H_2O
$$

**3. Iodoform test**

$$
CH_3OH + NaOH + I_2 \rightarrow \text{no reaction (iodoform not formed)}
$$

**4. Acetate ester formation**

$$
CH_3OH + CH_3COOH \xrightarrow{conc.\ H_2SO_4,\ \Delta} CH_3COOCH_3 + H_2O
$$

## Observations

| Test | Reagent/Condition | Observation | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Methyl salicylate test | Salicylic acid + conc. H₂SO₄, heat | Characteristic sweet smell of methyl salicylate [unclear in source: exact descriptor of the smell] | Confirms –OH, ester formed |
| Formaldehyde test | Red-hot Cu gauze, heat | Smell of HCHO | Methanol oxidised to formaldehyde |
| Iodoform test | I₂ + NaOH | No reaction, no smell | **Negative** — distinguishes MeOH from EtOH |
| Acetate ester formation | Anhydrous CH₃COOH + NaOH/KOH, or conc. H₂SO₄ + Δ | No smell reported | Methyl acetate formed, but source notes no characteristic smell |

## Notes / Observations from Source

- Iodine used in the iodoform test was resublimed.
- The exact sequencing of reagent addition in the acetate-formation step (NaOH/KOH vs. conc. H₂SO₄ pathway) is compactly written in the source; treated here as two related routes to the same ester.
- The negative iodoform result is the primary test distinguishing methanol from ethanol — see `methyl-vs-ethyl-alcohol-properties.md`.
